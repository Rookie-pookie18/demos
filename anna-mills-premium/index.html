Step 01 · Cleaning

First, the fieldcomes off.

Aspirators, destoners and magnets pull out straw, dust, stones and metal before the grain itself is touched.

14 cleaning stages · 99.8% foreign matter removed

Step 02 · Dehusking

The husk splitsin two.

Two rubber rollers spinning at different speeds peel the husk away without cracking the kernel inside.

Husk ≈ 20% of paddy weight · burned in our biomass boiler

Step 03 · Polishing

Brown becomespearl.

Friction whitening lifts off the bran layer; a fine water mist gives the grain its gloss.

Bran removed 8–10% · whiteness 42° Kett

Step 04 · Grading

Sorted bythe millimetre.

Indented-cylinder graders separate whole grains from brokens. Only kernels of 8.3 mm and longer go into our 1121 Basmati.

Broken grains ≤ 1% in export grade

Step 05 · Optical sorting

A camera seesevery grain.

Sorters inspect 40,000 grains a second and blow out chalky or discoloured ones with a single pulse of air.

Reject rate 0.6% · every lot traceable to its village

Since 1968

One mill,
four generations.

A two-stone huller on the Grand Trunk Road became one of Haryana's largest basmati exporters without ever leaving Karnal.

  1. 1968

    A two-stone huller in Karnal

    Milling paddy for neighbouring farms, paid in grain as often as in rupees.

  2. 1984

    First container to Dubai

    Twenty tonnes of hand-sorted basmati, shipped out of Bombay.

  3. 1999

    Steam parboiling plant

    Golden Sella joins the range for catering buyers in the Gulf.

  4. 2011

    Optical sorting lines

    Every grain now passes a camera before it's packed.

  5. 2018

    Powered by its own husk

    A biomass boiler burns the husk we peel off, supplying most of the mill's steam.

  6. 2025

    42 countries

    From Rotterdam to Riyadh, every bag traceable to the village it came from.
